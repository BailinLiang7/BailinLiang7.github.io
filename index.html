🛞Corner-Module Technology

A corner-module vehicle is a reconfigurable, over-actuated, and highly redundant vehicle architecture that can also serve as a versatile mobile robotic platform. Centered on in-wheel motors, it integrates drive, braking, steering, and suspension functions at each wheel end, enabling maneuvering modes beyond those of conventional vehicle architectures. This highly integrated configuration broadens the vehicle dynamics envelope, improves transmission efficiency, and enhances energy-saving potential.

🔬Research Experience

Coordinated Shimmy Suppression in Multi-Wheel Corner-Module Vehicles via Neural-Residual DiffTune-MPC

Sept 2026 - Present

Generalized Shimmy Dynamics Modeling: Developed a generalized nonlinear shimmy model for multi-wheel corner-module vehicles, capturing the inertial coupling among wheel-shimmy motion, suspension roll, and wheel rotational oscillations, together with lateral tire relaxation, steering backlash, and dry friction. Its modular architecture enables rapid adaptation across different multi-wheel corner-module chassis configurations.

Nonlinear Shimmy Dynamics Analysis: Investigating how steering backlash, dry friction, and dynamic coupling across multiple subsystems reshape stability boundaries and give rise to complex shimmy responses characterized by periodicity, quasiperiodicity, slow amplitude modulation, multistability, and nonzero-mean oscillations.

Shimmy-Aware Neural-Residual DiffTune-MPC: Developing a hybrid prediction model that augments a physics-guided nominal model with a neural residual to capture unmodeled nonlinear dynamics and reduce prediction mismatch. A shimmy-aware DiffTune-MPC scheme tunes the inner MPC cost weights against a closed-loop outer objective that minimizes band-limited shimmy energy and promotes oscillation-envelope contraction, enabling coordinated drive-brake-steer control for shimmy suppression.

Generalized Fault-Tolerant Control for Multi-Wheel Corner-Module Vehicles via Reconfigurable MPC

This work proposes a generalized fault-tolerant control framework for multi-wheel corner-module vehicles, enabling automated transfer across arbitrary multi-axle chassis configurations. By integrating automatic topology identification, health-aware MPC reconfiguration, and physical latent-space optimization, the framework maintains vehicle stability and tracking accuracy under diverse fault scenarios. Validation across 18 steering, driving, and composite fault scenarios demonstrates superior stability and tracking performance over baseline methods for both 4-wheel and 6-wheel configurations. Hardware-in-the-loop experiments verify real-time execution within 12.36 ms, confirming cross-configuration adaptability and practical deployability.

  • Proposed novel power-multiplexing electric wheel corner-module architectures to reduce structural redundancy arising from function-stacking in conventional designs.
